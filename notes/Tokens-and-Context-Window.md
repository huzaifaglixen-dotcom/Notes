# Tokens & Context Window — Deep Dive

Simple notes on two of the most important practical concepts when working with LLMs like GPT/Claude.

---

## 1. What is a Token?

**Simple definition:** A **token** is a small chunk of text — could be a whole word, part of a word, a punctuation mark, or even a space — that the AI model reads and generates, one piece at a time.

- AI models **don't read letters or full words directly** — they break text into tokens first
- Roughly: **1 token ≈ 4 characters ≈ ¾ of a word** in English (this varies by language/model)

### Example
The sentence: **"I love learning about AI!"**

Might get split into tokens like:
```
"I" | " love" | " learning" | " about" | " AI" | "!"
```
→ That's **6 tokens** for a 5-word sentence.

For a longer/unusual word:
```
"unbelievable" → "un" | "believ" | "able"
```
→ One word can become **multiple tokens** if it's complex or uncommon.

> This is why AI companies charge **per token** (not per word) — and why non-English languages sometimes use **more tokens** for the same sentence (because tokenization was mostly optimized around English/common patterns).

---

## 2. Why Tokens Matter

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Pricing** | API costs are usually calculated **per token** (input + output tokens) |
| **Speed** | More tokens = more processing = slower response |
| **Limits** | Every model has a **maximum number of tokens** it can handle at once (this is the Context Window — explained below) |
| **Understanding** | The model predicts the **next token**, one at a time, based on everything before it |

---

## 3. What is a Context Window?

**Simple definition:** The **Context Window** is the **maximum amount of text (in tokens)** that a model can "see" and consider at one time — including your entire conversation, prompt, and its own response.

Think of it as the model's **short-term memory limit** for a single conversation.

### Example
If a model has a **context window of 200,000 tokens** (roughly 150,000 words, or about a 500-page book):
- It can "remember" and refer back to everything within that limit — your messages, uploaded documents, its previous replies
- Once you go **beyond that limit**, the earliest parts of the conversation get **pushed out / forgotten** — the model literally can't see them anymore

---

## 4. Simple Analogy

Imagine the model's context window is like a **whiteboard of limited size**:

- Everything relevant to the current conversation is written on this whiteboard (your questions, its answers, any documents you shared)
- As long as it fits on the whiteboard, the model can "see" and use all of it
- But once the whiteboard is **full**, to write new stuff, the **oldest content at the top gets erased** to make room
- Anything erased is now **"forgotten"** by the model — it can't refer back to it anymore, even though it was said earlier in the same chat

> This is why in **very long conversations**, an AI might "forget" something you mentioned way earlier — it simply fell off the whiteboard (outside the context window).

---

## 5. Input Tokens vs Output Tokens

| Type | What It Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Input Tokens** | Everything you send to the model — your prompt, uploaded files, chat history | Your question + any pasted document |
| **Output Tokens** | Everything the model generates as a response | The AI's answer |

> Both count toward the **context window limit** and often toward **pricing** (many APIs charge input and output tokens at different rates).

---

## 6. Why This Matters Practically

| Situation | What Happens |
|---|---|
| **Uploading a huge document** | If it's too large, it might not fully fit in the context window along with your question |
| **Very long chat conversations** | Earlier messages may get "forgotten" once the context window fills up |
| **Asking for a very long response** | Uses more output tokens — costs more and takes longer |
| **Comparing AI models** | Some models have much larger context windows (e.g., can handle entire books), useful for tasks needing lots of reference material |

---

## 7. Key Terms (Quick Glossary)

| Term | Simple Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Token** | A small chunk of text (word/sub-word) the model reads or generates |
| **Tokenization** | The process of breaking text into tokens |
| **Context Window** | The maximum number of tokens a model can consider at once (its "memory limit" for a conversation) |
| **Input Tokens** | Tokens sent to the model (prompt, history, files) |
| **Output Tokens** | Tokens generated by the model (its response) |
| **Truncation** | When older parts of a conversation get cut off/dropped because the context window is full |

---

## One-Line Summary (for quick revision)

- **Token** → A small text chunk (roughly ¾ of a word) that AI models read/generate one at a time.
- **Context Window** → The maximum amount of text (tokens) a model can "see" at once — its short-term memory limit.
- **Analogy** → A whiteboard of limited size — once full, the oldest content gets erased to make room for new content.
- **Practical impact** → Affects pricing, speed, how much text/documents you can share, and whether the AI "remembers" earlier parts of a long conversation.
